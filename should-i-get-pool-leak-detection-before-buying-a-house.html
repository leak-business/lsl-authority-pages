Quick Answer

Yes. If the house has a pool, leak detection is one of the smartest things a buyer can do before closing. A pool can look fine during a showing and still have an active leak. The seller may have filled it before the appointment, the autofill may be hiding the water loss, or the leak may only show under specific operating conditions. A home inspection is not the same as a pool leak detection evaluation.

Why Pool Leak Detection Before Buying Makes Sense

A pool that looks beautiful during a showing can still have expensive problems. The autofill may be running constantly to replace what the pool is losing. Old patches may be failing at fittings, lights, or cracks in the shell. The spa may drain down every night through a fitting or plumbing failure. The equipment may have leaking unions or connections that are easy to miss on a visual walkthrough.

A general home inspector checks visible pool conditions - surface appearance, visible equipment, safety observations - but is not performing a systematic pool leak detection evaluation. Without a dive, dye testing, pipe testing, and acoustic evaluation, a pool inspection cannot confirm whether the pool is actively losing water or locate the source if it is.

At Leak and Subsurface Locators, a pre-purchase pool evaluation covers the pool shell, spa if present, equipment, skimmers, lights, fittings, drains, visible plumbing, previous repairs, and water loss clues. The goal is to give the buyer a clear picture of what they are inheriting before they close.

We prioritize real estate inspections and can often schedule within 1 to 2 days. Sometimes same day or next day if the schedule allows. Call as early in the inspection period as possible - a leak finding may lead to a repair estimate, seller negotiation, or a recheck before closing, all of which take time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the seller says the pool is fine and there are no leaks?

Seller representations are not a substitute for an independent evaluation. A pool can have active water loss that the seller is managing with an autofill - and may not even be aware of. An independent evaluation gives the buyer documented facts before the inspection period ends.
