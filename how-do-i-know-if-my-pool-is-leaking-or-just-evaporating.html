How Do I Know If My Pool Is Leaking or Just Evaporating?

The bucket test answers this question in 24 hours. Here is how to do it correctly and what the results actually mean.

Direct Answer

Do a bucket test. Fill a 5-gallon bucket with pool water and set it on a pool step so it sits partially submerged. Mark the water level inside the bucket and the pool level outside it. Turn off the autofill and wait 24 hours without adding water. If the pool dropped significantly more than the bucket, the pool is leaking. If both dropped the same amount, it is evaporation. In South Florida, normal evaporation is about one quarter inch per day â€” any consistent loss above that points to a leak.

Why the Bucket Test Works

Evaporation affects both the pool and the bucket equally. They are both exposed to the same sun, wind, and humidity. So if the pool is only evaporating, both should drop by the same amount over 24 hours.

A leak only affects the pool â€” not the bucket. So if the pool drops half an inch while the bucket only drops a quarter inch, the pool lost an extra quarter inch that cannot be explained by evaporation. That extra loss is the leak.

Partially submerging the bucket on a pool step equalizes the water temperature between the bucket and the pool, which makes the comparison more accurate. If the bucket sat outside the pool entirely, the temperature difference could skew the evaporation rates slightly.

Bucket Test â€” Step by Step

  1. Fill a 5-gallon bucket completely with pool water.
  2. Set the bucket on the top step of the pool so it sits in the water up to its rim â€” partially submerged.
  3. Use tape or a marker to mark the water level inside the bucket.
  4. Mark the pool water level on the outside of the bucket or on the pool wall at the waterline.
  5. Turn off the autofill completely. Do not add any water during the test.
  6. Wait 24 hours. Do not run any water features or backwash the filter during the test period.
  7. Compare the drop inside the bucket to the drop in the pool level. A significant difference means the pool is leaking.

How to Read the Results

Pool is leaking

The pool dropped noticeably more than the bucket â€” even half an inch more. The difference is the pool leaking. The larger the difference, the more the pool is losing.

Normal evaporation

Both the pool and the bucket dropped about the same amount. The loss is environmental â€” sun, wind, and heat â€” not a leak in the pool or plumbing.

Evaporation vs. Leak â€” The Key Differences

Signs of normal evaporation

  • Loss slows down after rain
  • Loss is higher in hot, windy weather
  • Pool and bucket drop the same amount
  • Loss is roughly consistent day to day
  • No wet spots in yard or near equipment

Signs of a leak

  • Loss continues even after rainy days
  • Pool drops faster overnight or with pump running
  • Pool drops much more than the bucket
  • Autofill runs constantly
  • Wet spots near pool, deck, or equipment pad
  • Water level stops dropping at a specific point

What Happens If the Autofill Is On During the Test?

The autofill must be turned off during the bucket test. If the autofill is running, it keeps replacing the water the pool is losing â€” so the pool level stays the same and the test cannot detect anything. Turn off the autofill valve or breaker before you mark the starting levels and leave it off for the full 24 hours.

This is the most common reason a bucket test gives a false result. Homeowners run the test with the autofill on and conclude the pool is fine because the level did not drop â€” when in reality the autofill replaced 400 gallons overnight and masked a significant leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I run the bucket test?

24 hours is the standard. Less than that and the difference may be too small to read accurately. You can run it for 48 hours for an even clearer result, especially if the loss is slow.

Should I run the pump during the bucket test?

Run the pump on its normal schedule. If you want to check whether the leak is worse with the pump on or off, run one 24-hour test with the pump running and one with it off. The difference between the two results tells you which side of the plumbing the leak is on.

What if the pool and bucket both dropped a lot?

If both dropped the same large amount, it was likely a very hot, windy day and the loss is evaporation. If you are concerned, repeat the test on a calmer day. If the pool consistently drops more than the bucket, it is leaking regardless of how much both dropped.

Can I skip the bucket test and just call a leak detection company?

Absolutely. If you already know the pool is losing too much water and you want it confirmed and located professionally, skip the bucket test and call us directly. We will assess it during the leak detection visit.
