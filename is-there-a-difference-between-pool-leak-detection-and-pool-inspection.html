Homeowners sometimes use these two terms interchangeably, but they describe different services with different tools, different scope, and different purposes. Understanding the difference helps you know which one to call for.

Pool Leak Detection

Pool leak detection is a diagnostic service focused on one specific question: why is this pool losing water, and where is it coming from? The technician uses a full diagnostic sequence -- acoustic listening, water pressure testing of plumbing lines, dye testing at every fitting, pipe locating, and trace gas when needed for underground breaks. The output is a confirmed finding: what is leaking, where it is, and what type of repair is needed.

You call for leak detection when your pool is losing more water than evaporation accounts for and you need to know the source.

Pool Inspection

A pool inspection evaluates the overall condition of the pool -- not just whether it is leaking but whether it is structurally sound, code-compliant, safe, and properly functioning. At Leak and Subsurface Locators, a real estate pool inspection covers: dive inspection of the shell and fittings, dye testing, pipe testing of pool plumbing lines, equipment functionality check, code violations, safety hazards, special concerns, and best practices.

You call for a pool inspection when buying or selling a home, when evaluating a pool's condition after a long period without use, or when you want a comprehensive condition assessment rather than a specific leak diagnosis.

Where They Overlap

Both services involve a dive inspection and dye testing. Both check pool fittings for active leaks. The difference is in scope: leak detection is narrowly focused on finding the water loss source, while an inspection covers the pool's entire condition and produces documentation relevant to condition assessment, real estate transactions, and maintenance planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a leak detection visit serve as a pool inspection for a real estate transaction?

The findings from a leak detection visit document what is leaking and where -- which is part of what a real estate buyer needs. But a full real estate pool inspection covers additional scope: equipment functionality, code compliance, safety features, and overall condition beyond just leak detection. If you need documentation for a real estate transaction, ask specifically for a pool inspection that covers the full real estate scope.

Does a pool inspection always include leak detection?

At LSL, yes -- the real estate pool inspection scope includes dye testing, pipe testing of pool plumbing lines, and the structural dive inspection. Not all pool inspection services include plumbing line testing, so ask any company you consider what their inspection scope covers before booking.

If a leak is found during a pool inspection, is that documented?

Yes. Any active leak findings identified during a pool inspection are documented in the inspection report -- location, type of leak, and what was confirmed. This documentation is valuable for a real estate buyer negotiating repairs or requesting credits before closing.
