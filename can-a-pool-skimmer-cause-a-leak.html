Direct Answer

Yes. Skimmer separation is one of the most common pool leak sources we find in South Florida. The plastic skimmer body gradually pulls away from the concrete pool shell at the bond between them â€” usually from soil movement, age, or repeated thermal expansion and contraction. Water drains through the gap until the waterline drops below the skimmer throat, at which point the leak effectively stops. The pool always stabilizes at the same level: the bottom of the skimmer opening.

Why Skimmer Separation Happens

Pool skimmers are plastic. Pool shells are concrete. These two materials move differently with temperature changes, soil movement, and the stress that comes from years of water pressure and ground settling. In South Florida's sandy soil and high-water-table environment, the ground around the skimmer shifts with the seasons. Over time the joint between the plastic skimmer body and the concrete shell develops a gap â€” often invisible from above the waterline but clearly visible during an underwater inspection.

Older pools built in the 1980s and 1990s are especially prone to skimmer separation because the original bonding materials age and lose elasticity. A pool that is 25 or 30 years old with the original skimmer has usually seen enough thermal cycling and ground movement for the joint to begin failing.

Signs your skimmer may be the leak source

  • Pool water level always drops to the same spot â€” the bottom of the skimmer opening
  • Pool loses water overnight but appears to stop dropping at skimmer level
  • Water loss is present with the pump off as well as on
  • A visible gap or crack where the skimmer meets the pool wall â€” sometimes seen from the deck looking into the skimmer basket area
  • Wet soil or staining on the exterior pool wall near the skimmer position

How We Confirm It

During the underwater inspection we go to each skimmer and apply leak detection dye at the throat, around the full perimeter of the skimmer body, and at the bottom pipe connection. If the skimmer is separating, the dye is drawn visibly through the gap â€” confirming active water movement at that exact location. We also pipe test the skimmer line to rule out a plumbing failure behind the skimmer as an additional source.
