What Separates a Good Pool Leak Detector From a Bad One?

A good one follows the evidence. A bad one guesses. Here is what that looks like in the field.

Good Leak Detector

  • Asks questions before testing
  • Evaluates the pool as a complete system
  • Tests in a logical order
  • Confirms findings when possible
  • Documents with photos and a written report
  • Explains findings in plain language
  • Honest about what cannot be confirmed

Bad Leak Detector

  • Jumps to conclusions without testing
  • Blames the most common leak source without proving it
  • Says "probably underground" without evidence
  • No written report or photos
  • Cannot explain the process step by step
  • Recommends repair before narrowing the leak

A skilled leak detector understands that the pool is a system. The water level, pump, valves, skimmer, lights, fittings, drains, spa, equipment, and plumbing all matter. The leak may show up only when the pump is on. It may show up only when the pump is off. It may stop at a certain level. It may be hidden by an autofill.

The H.U.N.T.E.R. Method

At LSL, we use the H.U.N.T.E.R. Method because it keeps us systematic. We hear the history, understand the layout, rule out non-leak causes, test the pool, expose the leak source when possible, and record the findings.
