Quick Answer

Sometimes yes, but it depends on access and timing. If the seller or listing agent allows access before an offer is made, we can inspect the pool. This can be useful if the pool shows visible warning signs - old patches, a draining spa, wet areas, visible cracks, or equipment leaks. In most real estate situations, the inspection happens during the inspection period after the offer is accepted, when access is more straightforward.

Pre-Offer Pool Evaluation - When It Works

A pre-offer pool evaluation is possible when the seller grants access before any contract is signed. This happens more often when the buyer is seriously interested, the pool shows visible warning signs, or the listing agent is willing to coordinate access for due diligence.

When a pre-offer evaluation makes sense: the pool has visible old patches or cracks, the spa appears to be draining down between visits, there are wet areas around the equipment pad or deck that are unexplained, the pool level looks low for no visible reason, or the equipment shows signs of leaks around unions or fittings.

The limitation of pre-offer evaluation is access. We need to reach the pool, equipment, hose, electrical outlet, and any controls needed for testing. If the seller is not cooperative, access may be limited or unavailable.

In most real estate transactions, the systematic pool evaluation happens during the inspection period after the offer is accepted. That is the more common path because the buyer has a formal window, access is expected, and the seller understands inspections are part of the process.

Whether you evaluate before or during the inspection period, the earlier you call us, the more time there is to get scheduled, produce the report, and allow time for any follow-up action before a deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I arrange pre-offer pool access?

Work with your real estate agent to request access from the listing agent. The seller needs to agree to allow the evaluation before any contract obligation exists. Your agent may frame it as a showing follow-up or a courtesy due diligence visit. Access logistics - gate codes, equipment room keys, who will be present - need to be arranged before we arrive.
