Is My Pool Leaking If the Autofill Keeps Running?

An autofill that runs every day is not maintaining your pool — it is hiding a leak. The pool looks full but it is losing water constantly and you are paying for every gallon.

Direct Answer

Yes. An autofill that runs constantly or clicks on every day is almost always masking a pool leak. It replaces the water the pool is losing so the level looks normal — but the pool is still leaking, the water bill is climbing, and whatever is causing the loss continues unchecked. Turn off the autofill for 24 hours and mark the water level. If the pool drops more than a quarter inch, it is leaking and the autofill was covering it up.

What an Autofill Actually Does

A pool autofill — also called an auto-leveler or automatic water leveler — is a float valve connected to your home's water supply. When the pool water drops below a set point, the float drops, the valve opens, and fresh water flows in until the level comes back up. Then it shuts off.

On a pool that is not leaking, the autofill should run only occasionally — after splashing, backwashing the filter, or a dry stretch without rain. It is not designed to run daily. When it runs constantly, the float valve is constantly compensating for water that the pool is continuously losing.

The autofill is not solving the problem — it is hiding it

Every gallon the autofill adds is a gallon the pool leaked. The leak does not stop because the autofill keeps up. It keeps going — eroding soil, washing out ground under the deck, diluting pool chemicals, and running up your water bill — until someone finds and fixes the actual source.

How to Confirm the Autofill Is Masking a Leak

The 24-Hour Autofill Test

  1. Locate the autofill valve — usually a small box set into the pool deck near the skimmer, or a float valve inside the skimmer.
  2. Turn the valve off completely so no fresh water can enter the pool.
  3. Mark the current water level on the pool wall or skimmer with tape.
  4. Wait 24 hours. Do not add any water during this period.
  5. Check the level. If the pool dropped more than a quarter inch, it is leaking. The autofill was replacing that loss every day without you knowing.

Signs Your Autofill Is Running Too Much

  • You can hear the autofill running for extended periods every day
  • Your water bill is higher than expected for the pool size
  • The pool chemistry is hard to maintain — constant fresh water dilutes chlorine and other chemicals
  • The autofill runs even after it has rained
  • The autofill never fully shuts off and you can always hear a trickle
  • Your water bill spiked suddenly with no other explanation

What Leaks Cause an Autofill to Run Constantly

Any active leak will cause the autofill to compensate. The most common sources on South Florida pools include skimmer separation, pool light conduit failures, cracks in the shell or tile line, failing return fittings, and underground plumbing breaks. A pool losing half an inch per day is losing roughly 750 gallons per week on a standard 15,000-gallon pool — all of it replaced silently by the autofill while the underlying damage continues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the autofill itself be broken and cause the pool to overflow?

Yes. A stuck float valve can cause the autofill to run continuously and overfill the pool — the opposite problem. If water is running over the edge constantly, check the float valve first. But if the pool level looks normal and the autofill still runs every day, the pool is leaking and the autofill is keeping up with it.

How much water loss causes the autofill to run every day?

It depends on how the float is set, but most autofills trigger when the pool drops a quarter inch or more below the set point. If the pool is leaking a quarter inch per day or more — which is above the normal evaporation rate for South Florida — the autofill will run every single day to compensate.

Is it okay to leave the autofill running while I wait to get the pool tested?

The autofill keeps the pool at a usable level, so it is not harmful to leave it running temporarily. But every day you wait, the leak continues. Underground leaks erode soil under decks and pool shells. The sooner the source is found, the less secondary damage accumulates.

Does a running autofill affect pool chemicals?

Yes. Constantly adding fresh water dilutes chlorine, pH adjusters, and other chemicals. Pools with an active leak that the autofill is masking are notoriously hard to keep in balance because the water chemistry is always being diluted. If your chemical usage has gone up for no apparent reason, water loss is a likely contributor.
