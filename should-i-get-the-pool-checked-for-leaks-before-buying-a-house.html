Should I Get the Pool Checked for Leaks Before Buying a House?

Yes — and your standard home inspection will not catch it. A pool that looks perfect during a showing can be losing hundreds of gallons a week behind the scenes.

Direct Answer

Yes. Every home buyer with a pool should have it checked for leaks during the inspection period. A standard home inspection does not include pressure testing the plumbing, underwater dye inspection, or any specialized leak testing. Pool leaks are often invisible during a showing — especially when the house has an autofill that keeps the water level looking normal. A pool leak assessment by a licensed pool contractor tells you whether the pool is leaking, where the leak is, and what it will cost to fix — before you close.

What a Standard Home Inspection Does and Does Not Cover

Standard home inspection — pool portion

  • Visual check of pool shell condition
  • Equipment pad observation
  • Check for visible cracks or damage
  • Observation of pump, filter, heater operation
  • Does NOT include pressure testing
  • Does NOT include underwater inspection
  • Does NOT include dye testing
  • Does NOT confirm whether pool is leaking

Pool leak assessment — what is included

  • Equipment pad inspection for active leaks
  • Visual and underwater inspection of full shell
  • Dye testing at fittings, lights, skimmer, drain
  • Pressure testing of all plumbing circuits
  • Underground locating if needed
  • Written report available for buyer, realtor, attorney
  • Findings explained clearly before leaving
  • Confirms whether pool is leaking and where

Why Pool Leaks Are Often Hidden During a Home Showing

Most South Florida pools have an automatic water leveler — an autofill connected to the house water supply. When the pool drops below a set level, the autofill opens and replaces the water. The pool always looks full. The level never visibly drops. Nothing appears wrong during a walk-through or showing.

The seller may not even know the pool is leaking. They notice the water bill is a bit high, maybe the pool chemicals are hard to balance, but the pool looks fine so they assume it is normal. Meanwhile the pool has been losing hundreds of gallons a week for months and the buyer is about to inherit the problem.

The only way to know is to turn off the autofill and test the pool — or have a licensed pool leak specialist assess it properly during the inspection period.

What Could Be Wrong That the Home Inspection Missed

  • Skimmer separation — extremely common on South Florida pools over 10 years old. The skimmer body pulls away from the concrete shell and drains continuously.
  • Underground return line crack — a pipe under the deck or yard that cracked from soil movement, root intrusion, or age. Invisible from above. Requires pressure testing to find.
  • Pool light conduit leak — the conduit running from the light niche through the pool wall. A failed seal here drains the pool down to the light level slowly and steadily.
  • Structural shell cracks — in gunite or plaster at or below the waterline. Some are visible from the deck. Many are not without underwater inspection.
  • Equipment pad plumbing leaks — failing unions, fittings, or valves at the pump, filter, or heater. Easy to miss on a visual walk-through if the area is enclosed or cluttered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I test the pool even if the seller says it is not leaking?

Yes. The seller may genuinely believe the pool is fine — especially if an autofill has been masking the loss. Claims of no leaks cannot be confirmed without testing. A pool assessment takes a few hours and costs far less than discovering a leak after you close.

Can you give me a written report my realtor can use?

Yes. We provide written reports documenting what was tested, what was found, and what we recommend. These reports are commonly used by buyers, sellers, realtors, real estate attorneys, and title companies as part of the inspection and negotiation process.

Can you recheck the pool after the seller makes repairs before closing?

Yes, if scheduling allows. A recheck visit confirms whether the specific leak we found was repaired properly. It gives the buyer confidence before closing that the repair was done correctly.

Do buyers need a licensed pool contractor or can any pool company do the assessment?

For a pool leak assessment that involves pressure testing plumbing lines, any work or recommendations related to repair should be performed by a licensed pool contractor. Leak and Subsurface Locators operates under Florida Certified Pool Contractor license CPC1457277.
