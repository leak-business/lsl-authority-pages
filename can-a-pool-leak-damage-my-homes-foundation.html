How a Pool Leak Can Reach the Foundation

Most pool plumbing runs underground from the pool shell to the equipment pad. On many South Florida properties, the equipment pad is located close to or against the house. When an underground pipe failure occurs along that plumbing run, the escaping water follows the path of least resistance through the sandy soil â€” which may lead directly toward the foundation.

The risk is not from a surface leak at the skimmer or a crack in the shell visible above the waterline. Those sources lose water into the pool area and drain into the surrounding soil, but typically not directly toward the foundation. The higher-risk scenario is a plumbing break in the lines that run between the pool and the house, especially when those lines pass under or near the slab.

Signs that sustained water loss may be affecting the structure

  • Doors or windows that have begun to stick, bind, or no longer close squarely
  • Gaps between walls and ceilings, or walls and floors
  • Visible cracks in exterior stucco on the pool side of the house
  • Soft, wet, or depressed areas in the lawn between the pool and the house

South Florida Soil and Water Table Factors

South Florida's sandy soil drains quickly but also loses structural support when waterlogged. Unlike clay soils that expand and contract dramatically with moisture, sandy soil erodes and voids form when significant water movement carries particles away from load-bearing areas under a slab. A significant underground plumbing failure â€” gallons per day over months â€” can create voids under the slab that eventually result in movement.

The high water table in much of Palm Beach and Broward County also means that additional water input from a pool leak can raise the local water table under the slab, further destabilizing the subgrade.
